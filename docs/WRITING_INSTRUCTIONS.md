# Writing Instructions.

This document defines the **writing style, narrative principles, and craft rules** an LLM should follow when writing literary fiction in this project’s style.  
It is **story‑agnostic** and applies regardless of setting, characters, or genre (as long as it is character‑driven fiction).

---

## CORE WRITING PHILOSOPHY

- **Character-first storytelling.** Plot emerges from characters’ choices, hesitations, and inner conflicts.
- **Interior over spectacle.** Emotional movement matters more than events.
- **Small moments > big declarations.** A glance, a pause, or a mundane action should carry meaning.
- **Trust the reader.** Do not over-explain emotions, motivations, or themes.

---

## NARRATIVE VOICE

- **Perspective:** Close third-person (limited), anchored tightly to one character at a time.
- **Distance:** Intimate but not confessional. The narrator observes thoughts without fully dissolving into first-person.
- **Voice:** Calm, observant, emotionally intelligent. Never preachy or dramatic.
- **Commentary:** Allowed sparingly—often to highlight what is *not* being said.

---

## EMOTIONAL WRITING RULES

- **Show, don’t label.**  
  Avoid:
  > “She felt conflicted.”  
  Prefer:
  > She stared at the message, typed a reply, deleted it, then locked her phone face down.

- **Ambiguity is strength.**  
  Characters may not fully understand themselves. Let contradictions coexist.

- **Emotion unfolds in layers.**  
  Rarely resolve feelings in a single scene. Let them echo across scenes.

---

## DIALOGUE STYLE

- Natural, imperfect, and context-aware.
- Characters:
  - Interrupt themselves
  - Change topics mid-sentence
  - Avoid saying the most important thing directly
- Use silence, pauses, and body language as dialogue.

**Avoid:**
- Exposition dumps in dialogue
- Overly clever or theatrical speech
- Characters explaining their emotions clearly and fully

---

## SCENE CONSTRUCTION

Each scene should:
1. Be grounded in a **specific physical setting**
2. Contain at least one **sensory detail** (touch, smell, light, texture)
3. Advance **emotional state**, not just plot
4. End with:
   - A question
   - A shift
   - Or a quiet unresolved note

Scenes do **not** need:
- A clear beginning-middle-end
- Explicit conflict
- A takeaway message

---

## PACING & STRUCTURE

- Prefer **chronological flow**, often day-by-day.
- Let mundane actions breathe (commutes, meals, routines).
- Vary sentence length:
  - Short sentences for emotional impact
  - Longer ones for reflection and atmosphere
- Avoid rushing through emotionally significant moments.

---

## THEMATIC HANDLING

- Themes should **emerge**, not be stated.
- Social commentary is acceptable **only when embedded naturally** in:
  - Conversation
  - Observation
  - Internal reflection
- Never argue a position directly. Let characters embody tensions and contradictions.

---

## CHARACTER WRITING GUIDELINES

- Characters are:
  - Intelligent but imperfect
  - Capable of kindness and mistakes simultaneously
  - Often unaware of how they appear to others
- Secondary characters exist to:
  - Reflect, challenge, or soften the protagonist
  - Not to deliver moral lessons or plot convenience

---

## SENSORY DETAIL USE

Use sensory details:
- Sparingly but precisely
- To anchor emotion in the body

Examples:
- Temperature changes
- Fabric against skin
- Lingering scents
- Light at different times of day

Avoid purple prose or decorative excess.

---

## TEXTING / DIGITAL COMMUNICATION

- Messages should feel casual and real.
- Digital conversations:
  - Can interrupt scenes
  - Can heighten anxiety or intimacy
- Formatting can be inline or lightly separated, but should never feel gimmicky.

---

## WHAT TO AVOID

❌ Over-explaining motivations  
❌ Moralizing or instructive narration  
❌ Sudden personality shifts without buildup  
❌ Using trauma as shock value  
❌ Excessive metaphors  
❌ Clean emotional resolution too quickly  

---

## WHAT TO PRIORITIZE

✅ Emotional continuity  
✅ Internal conflict over external drama  
✅ Relationships evolving through small interactions  
✅ Respectful, nuanced portrayal of identity and selfhood  
✅ Writing that feels lived-in, not constructed  

---

## FINAL CHECK BEFORE WRITING

Ask:
- Would a real person think this way?
- Is this moment earned?
- Am I explaining something the reader could infer?
- Does this scene *feel* true, even if nothing dramatic happens?

If unsure, **simplify**.
